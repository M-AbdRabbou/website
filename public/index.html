About

I work on how quantum systems hold, lose and transfer information. My research sits between quantum information theory, quantum optics and open-system dynamics, with recent work on quantum thermodynamic machines, quantum batteries, and information scrambling.

I completed my Ph.D. in quantum information at Al-Azhar University in 2020 under Prof. A.-S. F. Obada, and I have taught in its Department of Mathematics since 2014, as a lecturer since 2021. Since 2024 I have been a postdoctoral researcher at the School of Physics, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, working with Prof. Cong-Feng Qiao.

A recent direction in my group applies machine learning to quantum state characterisation: stacked ensemble classifiers for entanglement detection in high-spin systems, and neural surrogate models that replace expensive statistical sampling of entanglement in light-matter systems.

Research interests

The common thread is the behaviour of quantum correlations once a system is coupled to something it cannot control: a thermal bath, a classical noise field, an accelerating frame, or a curved spacetime.

Entanglement & quantum steering Entropic uncertainty relations Open quantum systems and decoherence Quantum thermodynamics & Otto/Carnot cycles Quantum batteries Quantum information scrambling Relativistic quantum information Cavity QED & atom-field interaction Quantum Fisher information & metrology Magnomechanics & optomechanics Machine learning for quantum states Quantum game theory
